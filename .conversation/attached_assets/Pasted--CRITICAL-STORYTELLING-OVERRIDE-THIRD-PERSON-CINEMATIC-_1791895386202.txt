# CRITICAL STORYTELLING OVERRIDE — THIRD-PERSON CINEMATIC NARRATION

Modify the entire storytelling approach of the website "My M ❤️".

The story must NOT be narrated by Mohamed himself throughout the experience. Instead, the main story must be told by an independent, cinematic third-person narrator who tells the story of a man whose name begins with M and the woman who unexpectedly changed his life.

The narrator is an invisible storyteller, like the narrator of a beautiful animated movie or an emotional cinematic fairytale. The narrator speaks directly to the visitor at certain moments, but describes Mohamed as "he", "that man", or "the man whose name begins with M", rather than using "I" when describing his life.

The girl should gradually realize that the story is about the man who loves her, and that she is the girl who changed his world.

## 1. NARRATOR'S VOICE AND PERSONALITY

The narrator should sound:
- Warm, intimate, and emotionally intelligent.
- Poetic without sounding artificial or excessively dramatic.
- Mysterious during the opening.
- Slightly playful when describing the unexpected circumstances of their meeting.
- Deeply emotional during the moon-and-sea scene.
- Reassuring and sincere when describing his intentions and commitment.
- Cinematic, as if telling the story of two people whose lives unexpectedly crossed.

The narrator speaks in natural Egyptian Arabic, not formal literary Arabic.

The narration should feel like someone who knows the story and is gently revealing it to the girl, one secret at a time.

Do not make the narrator sound like Mohamed speaking in disguise. The narrator is a separate storyteller.

## 2. NARRATION RULES

Throughout the main story:
- Replace first-person descriptions of Mohamed's past with third-person narration.
- Refer to Mohamed as "هو", "الشاب ده", "الراجل ده", or "الشخص اللي اسمه بيبدأ بحرف M", depending on the scene.
- Address the girl as "إنتِ" when it makes the story more personal and emotionally engaging.
- Do not repeatedly announce Mohamed's full identity. Let the letter M remain a subtle mystery and recurring signature.
- Keep the original facts of their story intact.
- Do not invent memories, conversations, promises, or events that were never provided.
- Do not make the narrator omniscient about the girl's private thoughts. Describe only what is known or present possibilities without claiming certainty.
- Preserve the Egyptian dialect and conversational warmth.
- Keep the story coherent across all scenes, with each scene revealing something new.

## 7. THE MOON-AND-SEA SCENE — KEEP HIS ICONIC WORDS PERSONAL

The main story is told by the third-person narrator, but this scene has a special rule.

The narrator introduces the metaphor:

"وفيه جملة كان بيقولها..."

"جملة بسيطة، لكنها كانت بتشرح إحساس جواه بطريقة ما يعرفش يقولها بأي كلام تاني."

Then display Mohamed's iconic sentence exactly as he says it:

"بيقولك إيه بقى، لو حبيت بتبقى عامل زي الشخص اللي قاعد لوحده وشاف انعكاس القمر على البحر بليل، وافتكر إن القمر نزل يقعد عشان يونسه."

The sentence must feel like his own voice, not the narrator's invented quotation. Present it as his personal signature, with a subtle visual transition into a moonlit ocean.

After the quote, the narrator resumes:

"يمكن هو ما كانش يقصد القمر لوحده..."

"يمكن كان بيتكلم عن الإحساس إن حاجة بعيدة تبقى قريبة من القلب بشكل ما يتوصفش."

"وعن إن الواحد، حتى لو كان متعود يمشي لوحده، ممكن يقابل شخص يخلّيه يتمنى إن الطريق يفضل يجمعهم."

"ويمكن دي كانت طريقته في إنه يقولها، من غير ما يضطر يشرح كل اللي جواه..."

"إن وجودها بقى فارق معاه."

Do not change the original quote into a different sentence. The narrator may interpret its emotional meaning afterward, but must not claim to know thoughts beyond the story.
